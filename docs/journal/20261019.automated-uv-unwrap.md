### Goal

Unwrap FSS cockpit parts automatically, with the best possible result and as little hand work as possible. Textures are made in Substance Painter (3D painting, smart materials), so seam placement matters less than in 2D painting; it still matters for direction-based materials, texel density, mip bleed, labels, and the vertex count (every UV seam splits vertices, which MSFS counts toward its LOD vertex limit).

### Research Summary

- **Blender 5.2.2 built-ins** (checked): Smart UV Project (cuts by angle, projects per group), Unwrap (Angle Based, Conformal, Minimum Stretch), Pack Islands (concave shapes, any rotation, pinning, UDIM regions), Average Islands Scale, Select Overlap, Seams from Islands. No real automatic seam placement.
- **xatlas** (PyPI bindings MIT; core library MIT from memory, not checked): automatic chart growing by a heuristic cost, LSCM, packing. The bindings take no material IDs (`face_materials` only on `add_uv_mesh`) and only per-vertex normals.
- **RizomUV 2027** (released 8 October 2026): headless Python mode on Windows, macOS, Linux; driven from Blender through RizomUVLink (MIT). Commercial per artist (Indie VS 149.90 EUR perpetual). Optional backend at most, never required.
- **UVPackmaster 4** (commercial packer), **Ministry of Flat** (commercial, command line): not needed so far.
- **Learned seam placement** (SeamFlow, SeamGen, SeamCrafter, MeshQuery, 2025 to 2026): research; weights and licences unclear, about 100k faces per mesh, trained on general models. Not usable now.
- **PartUV** (SIGGRAPH Asia 2025, github.com/EricWang12/PartUV, Apache 2.0, source read): a binary part tree over faces, searched top down; per node, candidates (box-direction charts merged greedily, normal clustering) are unwrapped (ABF++ / LSCM) and the candidate with the fewest charts under a distortion threshold (default 1.25, mean per-face area distortion) wins; children are tried only if splitting yields fewer charts. Its part tree comes from NVIDIA's PartField, which is under a **non-commercial NVIDIA licence** (`preprocess_utils/partfield_official`) and must not be used. The build requires CUDA (only for a GPU mesh simplification that speeds up distortion checks). Taken over as ideas: fewest charts under one distortion limit, merges accepted only after a real unwrap with no overlap, box-direction starting charts, boundary smoothing.

### Prototype (scratch only, not in the repository)

- Pipeline per mesh: forced cuts, soft starting charts (angle about 30 degrees, plus box-direction charts in smooth areas), verified greedy merging (smallest chart into the neighbour with the longest shared border; accepted only if `bpy.ops.uv.unwrap(ANGLE_BASED)` of the two charts keeps the PartUV area distortion under 1.25 and creates no UV overlap), strip absorption, boundary smoothing with re-check, worst-triangle pass, a repair step for pieces never checked, Average Islands Scale, Pack Islands (concave). Minimum Stretch did not reproduce the trial results in the final whole-mesh unwrap, so Angle Based is used.
- **Round 1** (forced cuts at edges over 60 degrees and material borders), **round 2** (forced cuts at shading splits), **round 3** (forced cuts at artist-marked seams and material borders; shading splits soft), on `COCKPIT.blend` and on `E2_190_COCKPIT2.blend` (the E2 cockpit with seams marked by an artist: 669 of 2,526 meshes carry 77,294 seam edges).
- **Round 3 islands** (artist / round 3 / Smart UV 66 degrees / xatlas): knob 10 / 10 / 25 / 24; `TRIM_PANEL` 53 / 74 / 234 / 267; pedestal 207 / 226 / 427 / 482; floor shell 367 / 604 / 2,137 / 1,961; overhead shell 778 / 1,030 / 3,356 / 3,964. Round 3: no UV overlaps; texel density uniform; artist seams respected (remaining continuous UVs only at dead-end seam stubs); mean area distortion 1.05 to 1.12, worst triangles up to 3 to 4 on the large shells; packing 30 to 56 percent of the square. The floor shell's main island matches the artist's main island closely.
- **Measured costs:** 0.4 s (knob) to about 100 s (floor shell, 24k triangles), mostly box-split checks, merging and packing.
- **Remaining weak points:** thin strips and tiny pieces that are bounded entirely by seams or open borders (cannot merge) or fail the distortion limit; hollow frame and lattice islands; packing efficiency on the large shells.
- **Findings:**
  - Cutting at shading splits costs almost no MSFS vertices (MSFS splits the vertex there anyway) but yields many islands; freeing them roughly halves islands, with mixed vertex cost.
  - Smart UV Project overlapped on several parts; xatlas reached worst triangles of 58 to 180 on the shells.
  - In `E2_190_COCKPIT2.blend` many existing UV maps do not follow the marked seams (only 289 objects follow them exactly).
- Prototype files (machine-local, not committed): the agent's scratchpad `uvproto\` (`proto_blender_v3.py`, `uvmetrics.py`, `evaluate3.py`, `results3\table.md`, layout images).

### Maintainer Decisions

1. **No normal maps baked from a high-poly model for the E2 cockpit** (the `.blend` is the high poly). Shading splits (marked sharp edges, Weighted Normal and Auto Smooth splits) are therefore not UV cuts; islands may merge across them.
2. **Artist-marked seams are the artist's intent** and are honoured as forced cuts. Marked sharp edges are not seams. When existing UVs disagree with the marked seams, the seams win (the UVs may be stale or not yet unwrapped).
3. **Stacking only from explicit signals:** objects sharing one mesh datablock, and Mirror (and Array) modifiers, share UV space. Identical shapes with their own mesh data are never stacked automatically (the artist may want variation). Consequence: unwrap once per mesh datablock, on the original mesh before Mirror and Array, never on the evaluated or merged mesh; the overlap check ignores overlaps that come from shared data or mirroring.
4. **Texel density per object (node):** default 1:1 with the object's surface area. The artist can mark an object for higher density (example: glareshield) or lower density (example: rear door). Later, an automatic visibility test may lower the density of objects rarely visible from the normal camera views.
5. **Explicit in the UI:** the tool either recreates the UVs of the chosen objects or does nothing; it never overwrites UVs implicitly (as decision 26 for polygon reduction in the LOD journal).
6. Material borders stay forced cuts (separate texture sets); in the tested objects they contributed no cuts.
