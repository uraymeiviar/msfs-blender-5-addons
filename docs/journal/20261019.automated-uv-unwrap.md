## Session 1: Research, Prototype, Maintainer Decisions

- **Date:** 20261019
- **Developer:** uraymeiviar
- **Agent:** Claude Code (Opus 5.5), prototype rounds run by a Claude sub-agent
- **Branch:** main

### Goal

Unwrap FSS cockpit parts automatically, with the best possible result and as little hand work as possible. Textures are made in Substance Painter (3D painting, smart materials), so seam placement matters less than in 2D painting; it still matters for direction-based materials, texel density, mip bleed, labels, and the vertex count (every UV seam splits vertices, which MSFS counts toward its LOD vertex limit).

### Research Summary

- **Blender 5.2.2 built-ins** (checked): Smart UV Project (cuts by angle, projects per group), Unwrap (Angle Based, Conformal, Minimum Stretch), Pack Islands (concave shapes, any rotation, pinning, UDIM regions), Average Islands Scale, Select Overlap, Seams from Islands. No real automatic seam placement.
- **xatlas** (PyPI bindings MIT; core library MIT from memory, not checked): automatic chart growing by a heuristic cost, LSCM, packing. The bindings take no material IDs (`face_materials` only on `add_uv_mesh`) and only per-vertex normals.
- **RizomUV 2027** (released 8 October 2026): headless Python mode on Windows, macOS, Linux; driven from Blender through RizomUVLink (MIT). Commercial per artist (Indie VS 149.90 EUR perpetual). Optional backend at most, never required.
- **UVPackmaster 4** (commercial packer), **Ministry of Flat** (commercial, command line): not needed so far.
- **Learned seam placement** (SeamFlow, SeamGen, SeamCrafter, MeshQuery, 2025 to 2026): research; weights and licences unclear, about 100k faces per mesh, trained on general models. Not usable now.
- **PartUV** (SIGGRAPH Asia 2025, github.com/EricWang12/PartUV, Apache 2.0, source read): a binary part tree over faces, searched top down; per node, candidates (box-direction charts merged greedily, normal clustering) are unwrapped (ABF++ / LSCM) and the candidate with the fewest charts under a distortion threshold (default 1.25, mean per-face area distortion) wins; children are tried only if splitting yields fewer charts. Its part tree comes from NVIDIA's PartField, which is under a **non-commercial NVIDIA licence** (`preprocess_utils/partfield_official`) and must not be used. The build requires CUDA (only for a GPU mesh simplification that speeds up distortion checks). Taken over as ideas: fewest charts under one distortion limit, merges accepted only after a real unwrap with no overlap, box-direction starting charts, boundary smoothing.

### Prototype (scratch only, not in the repository)

- Pipeline per mesh: forced cuts, soft starting charts (angle about 30 degrees, plus box-direction charts in smooth areas), verified greedy merging (smallest chart into the neighbour with the longest shared border; accepted only if `bpy.ops.uv.unwrap(ANGLE_BASED)` of the two charts keeps the PartUV area distortion under 1.25 and creates no UV overlap), strip absorption, boundary smoothing with re-check, worst-triangle pass, a repair step for pieces never checked, Average Islands Scale, Pack Islands (concave). Minimum Stretch did not reproduce the trial results in the final whole-mesh unwrap, so Angle Based is used.
- **Round 1** (forced cuts at edges over 60 degrees and material borders), **round 2** (forced cuts at shading splits), **round 3** (forced cuts at artist-marked seams and material borders; shading splits soft), on `COCKPIT.blend` and on `E2_190_COCKPIT2.blend` (the E2 cockpit with seams marked by an artist: 669 of 2,526 meshes carry 77,294 seam edges).
- **Round 3 islands** (artist / round 3 / Smart UV 66 degrees / xatlas): knob 10 / 10 / 25 / 24; `TRIM_PANEL` 53 / 74 / 234 / 267; pedestal 207 / 226 / 427 / 482; floor shell 367 / 604 / 2,137 / 1,961; overhead shell 778 / 1,030 / 3,356 / 3,964. Round 3: no UV overlaps; texel density uniform; artist seams respected (remaining continuous UVs only at dead-end seam stubs); mean area distortion 1.05 to 1.12, worst triangles up to 3 to 4 on the large shells; packing 30 to 56 percent of the square. The floor shell's main island matches the artist's main island closely.
- **Measured costs:** 0.4 s (knob) to about 100 s (floor shell, 24k triangles), mostly box-split checks, merging and packing.
- **Remaining weak points:** thin strips and tiny pieces that are bounded entirely by seams or open borders (cannot merge) or fail the distortion limit; hollow frame and lattice islands; packing efficiency on the large shells.
- **Findings:**
  - Cutting at shading splits costs almost no MSFS vertices (MSFS splits the vertex there anyway) but yields many islands; freeing them roughly halves islands, with mixed vertex cost.
  - Smart UV Project overlapped on several parts; xatlas reached worst triangles of 58 to 180 on the shells.
  - In `E2_190_COCKPIT2.blend` many existing UV maps do not follow the marked seams (only 289 objects follow them exactly).
- Prototype files (machine-local, not committed): the agent's scratchpad `uvproto\` (`proto_blender_v3.py`, `uvmetrics.py`, `evaluate3.py`, `results3\table.md`, layout images).

### Maintainer Decisions

1. **No normal maps baked from a high-poly model for the E2 cockpit** (the `.blend` is the high poly). Shading splits (marked sharp edges, Weighted Normal and Auto Smooth splits) are therefore not UV cuts; islands may merge across them.
2. **Artist-marked seams are the artist's intent** and are honoured as forced cuts. Marked sharp edges are not seams. When existing UVs disagree with the marked seams, the seams win (the UVs may be stale or not yet unwrapped).
3. **Stacking only from explicit signals:** objects sharing one mesh datablock, and Mirror (and Array) modifiers, share UV space. Identical shapes with their own mesh data are never stacked automatically (the artist may want variation). Consequence: unwrap once per mesh datablock, on the original mesh before Mirror and Array, never on the evaluated or merged mesh; the overlap check ignores overlaps that come from shared data or mirroring.
4. **Texel density per object (node):** default 1:1 with the object's surface area. The artist can mark an object for higher density (example: glareshield) or lower density (example: rear door). Later, an automatic visibility test may lower the density of objects rarely visible from the normal camera views.
5. **Explicit in the UI:** the tool either recreates the UVs of the chosen objects or does nothing; it never overwrites UVs implicitly (as decision 26 for polygon reduction in the LOD journal).
6. Material borders stay forced cuts (separate texture sets); in the tested objects they contributed no cuts.

### Maintainer Decisions (continued)

7. **Auto-seam, unwrap and packing are separate phases and separate tools.** Each runs on its own and can be repeated; the artist can step in between them (edit the proposed seams, then unwrap; adjust islands, then pack).

### Phase Design

| Phase | Input | Output | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Auto-seam** | mesh (original data, before Mirror/Array), artist seams, material borders, density and other marks | seam edges | Uses trial unwraps internally only to verify merges; writes seams, not UVs. |
| **2. Unwrap** | mesh with seams (artist and automatic) | UV islands, flattened, no overlaps | Angle Based (Minimum Stretch did not reproduce trial results); verification of overlap and distortion; worst-triangle repair. |
| **3. Packing** | islands of all objects in a texture set | packed UV layout | Texel density per object (decision 4), stacking from shared data and Mirror (decision 3), margins per texture resolution, texture sets per material. Own algorithm, planned separately. |

- **Artist seams versus automatic seams:** Blender has one seam flag per edge. The auto-seam phase must record which seams it wrote (for example a boolean edge attribute such as `fss_auto_seam`, set together with the seam flag), so that a re-run replaces only its own seams and every other seam stays an artist seam (decision 2). The name and storage are to be decided in the implementation design.
- Each phase is explicit in the UI, recreate or nothing (decision 5).

### Auto-Seam Optimization Plan (before the comparison with a finished artist file)

Ordered by expected gain; each item is measured with the round-3 metrics (islands, mean and worst distortion, overlaps, seam length, extra vertices beyond shading splits, run time) on the same test objects.

1. **Search instead of greedy merging:** PartUV's tree search, with splits kept only when they pay (fewer charts under the distortion limit); a part tree from the object hierarchy, loose pieces and normal or sharp-edge clustering (no PartField). Option: optimise seams and distortion jointly (OptCuts-style).
2. **Seams where nobody looks:** score candidate cut edges by visibility from the normal camera views (the same visibility test planned for texel density, decision 4) and by concavity; prefer hidden and concave edges, back sides and edges along existing edge loops.
3. **Clean island borders:** straight seams along edge loops instead of saw-tooth borders (the round-3 smoothing rejected most of its moves).
4. **Thin strips:** a size-dependent distortion limit for small strips, or attach and relax with a stronger solver, so strips join a neighbour.
5. **Hollow frames and lattices:** a verified cut into straight pieces for every frame and lattice shape (round 3 handled simple frames only).
6. **Comparison with RizomUV's automatic seams** (trial licence), to see whether a commercial tool is clearly better on these parts.
7. **Later:** a model trained on FSS artists' own seams (no licence problem), once enough finished seam data exists.
8. **When a fully seamed artist file exists:** remove the artist seams on objects whose UVs follow them, run the auto-seam phase, and compare its seams with the artist's (edge precision and recall, island count); tune the parameters on that.

- An artist test in Substance Painter on two or three parts judges what the numbers cannot.

## Session 4: Packing (Phase 3) Initial Design

- **Date:** 20261019
- **Developer:** uraymeiviar

### MSFS Texture Facts (SDK, local copy `F:\new-fss-aircraft-ejet\doc\msfs-sdk\`)

- **Sizes:** texture dimensions **must be a multiple of 4 pixels**, at least 8 × 8; textures need not be square (`pages/models-and-textures/textures/texturing-technical-information.md`). No power-of-two requirement; the DevMode Material Inspector page still says "power of 2", the dedicated texturing page is taken as current. The maintainer has used 8K textures.
- **Budget:** a large aircraft (cockpit included) is budgeted at about 600 MB of textures; KTX2 on disk is about what the GPU holds (`pages/models-and-textures/modeling/aircraft/airframe/airframe-textures-and-materials.md`). Estimate, not measured: one 8K map with mips is about 85 MB, so an 8K material with three maps is about 250 MB. Packing efficiency saves memory directly.
- **No UDIM:** the SDK never mentions UDIM, and a glTF material samples one texture per map (inferred from the SDK's silence, not confirmed). "UDIM" here therefore means splitting an overflowing material into tiles at export, each tile its own material and texture set (one more material and draw call per tile).

### Maintainer Decisions (continued)

8. **One atlas per material** across all objects using it; UDIM-like tiles are allowed for overflow.
9. **Texture size up to 8K,** any multiple of 4 (not limited to powers of two).
10. **Overflow:** when a material's islands do not fit at the target density, either split into tiles or scale down (optional).
11. **Rotation:** free rotation by default; the artist marks objects that have an explicit direction (brushed metal, grain, text), and their islands keep their orientation.

### Packing Pipeline (proposed)

1. **Texture sets:** group islands by material.
2. **Scale:** each island to its 3D area × the object's density factor (decision 4: 1.0 by default, artist marks higher or lower, later the visibility test).
3. **Stacking units:** islands from shared mesh data and Mirror or Array (decision 3) are packed as one unit.
4. **Constraints:**
   - directional objects keep upright (0 or 180 degrees, or 90-degree steps where allowed);
   - pinned islands keep their place.
5. **Placement on a pixel grid at the target resolution** (as the xatlas packer does):
   - rasterize each island grown by the margin in pixels;
   - place the largest first, at the most compact free position;
   - let small islands fill holes inside large ones.

   This handles concave shapes, holes and exact pixel margins. First version in Python with numpy; native module later if slow.
6. **Margins** in pixels from the texture resolution and the mip levels that must stay clean (for example 8 px at 8K; rule to be tuned), not a fixed UV fraction.
7. **Overflow:**
   - **Scale down:** uniform, reporting the density lost.
   - **Tiles:** split into tiles at export, reporting the extra materials.
8. **Optional best-quality pass:** an external irregular nesting engine when the grid packer leaves too much space empty (see candidates).
9. **Metrics:**
   - share of the area used;
   - smallest margin in pixels;
   - texel density spread against the targets;
   - island bleeding at a chosen mip level;
   - texture memory;
   - run time.

### Task 2: Grid Packer Prototype (`pack_grid.py`)

Implement the Session 4 pipeline as a stand-alone script (numpy; venv Python or Blender's Python), per texture set:

1. **Density scaling:** scale every island so that its UV area / 3D area equals the target density × the object's density factor. Target density follows from the texture size and a chosen pixels-per-metre; density factors come from a small JSON file (default 1.0). For the test, set one object to 2.0 and one to 0.5.
2. **Directional objects:** islands of objects listed as directional in the same JSON may only rotate by 0 or 180 degrees. Mark one test object directional. All other islands may rotate freely (test at least 8 to 16 candidate angles, or principal-axis alignment plus 90-degree steps).
3. **Stacking units:** islands that must overlap on purpose (shared mesh data, Mirror) are placed as one unit. The round-3 objects have no such cases (they are evaluated copies). Build a small synthetic test instead: one object with a Mirror modifier, unwrapped before the modifier, and two objects sharing one mesh. Verify that their islands stay stacked after packing.
4. **Placement:** rasterize each island at the target resolution, grown by the margin in pixels, and place islands largest first at the most compact free position, letting small islands fill holes inside large ones. Full 8K bitmaps may be slow: a coarse-to-fine scheme (place on a coarse grid, verify and nudge at full resolution) is acceptable. State the scheme used.
5. **Overflow** (decision 10), selectable:
   - **scale:** shrink all islands of the set uniformly until they fit, and report the density lost;
   - **tiles:** fill tile 0, then continue in tile 1 and so on, and report the number of tiles (each tile becomes its own material at export later).
6. **Margins:** in pixels. Test 4 and 8 px at 4K, and 8 and 16 px at 8K.

### Metrics per Texture Set and Method

- share of the square used (island area / tile area);
- the smallest distance between islands in pixels, and the islands closer than the margin (must be 0);
- overlaps (must be 0, except deliberate stacking units);
- texel density per island against its target (max deviation; must be within 1 % unless overflow scaling applied);
- directional islands' rotation (must be 0 or 180 degrees);
- islands touching each other at mip levels 1 to 4 (a margin of m px survives about log2(m) mip levels; report the first level at which islands merge);
- tiles used or scale factor applied;
- estimated texture memory per set (three maps, compressed with mips);
- run time.

### Pass Criteria

- **Hard requirements:** 0 margin violations and 0 overlaps (stacking units excepted); density within 1 % of target; directional islands not rotated.
- **Packing efficiency:** the grid packer uses at least as much of the square as Blender's Pack Islands on every texture set, at both resolutions.
- **Run time:** no more than 60 s per texture set at 8K on the largest set.

### Implemented Experiments

All five switches are independent and off by default. Baseline settings and
packing remain unchanged except when a named experiment alters chart topology.

1. `tree`: normal-cluster binary tree rooted in loose/forced-cut components;
   centroid median breaks ties. A verified parent replaces children only when it
   has fewer connected charts under the mean-distortion limit and no overlap.
   Parent trials are capped at 512 faces; larger nodes retain children. This is
   bounded search, not a claim of PartUV equivalence or a global minimum. The
   baseline strip pass still follows tree selection.
2. `visibility`: three outward hemisphere rays per face estimate self-occlusion.
   Candidate cut length is weighted by exposure, signed concavity, and collinear
   edge continuation. High-cost borders are preferred for removal during merging.
   No pilot-camera visibility or other scene occluders were measured.
3. `borders`: local two-edge boundary-path replacements are accepted only when
   length/edge-continuation cost decreases, followed by the baseline real unwrap
   and overlap re-verification. It is local path simplification on the triangulated
   mesh, not arbitrary-length global loop rerouting.
4. `strips`: allowance rises from 1.25 by up to 0.10 as a strip's area fraction
   decreases below 0.001. Every merge still requires an overlap-free trial;
   object-level mean/worst distortion gates remain separate acceptance criteria.
5. `frames`: extend candidates to every inner boundary, with one/two/four starts
   per hole and penalties for turning away from edge chains. Reject any candidate
   that worsens the chart's maximum distortion. This attempts multi-hole lattices;
   unsuccessful decompositions remain failures rather than being declared solved.

`--phase seams` skips packing, exports proposed edge pairs, records ownership in
`fss_auto_seam`, removes the temporary UV layer, and asserts that original UV maps
are unchanged. Benchmark mode explicitly exports UVs for the unchanged evaluator.
Neither mode saves a blend file. The attribute name is prototype-local, not a final
add-on storage design decision.

### Adoption and Remaining Failures

- **No switch is adopted as a default.** No single switch, and not the five combined, passes the Session 3 criteria on every object. All switches stay in `proto_blender_v4.py`, off by default.
- **Hard requirements hold everywhere:** every variant has 0 UV overlaps and 0 artist-seam crossings outside dead-end stubs on all seven objects.
- **Where the switches help:** the combination of all five reduces `COCKPIT_SHELL_FLOOR` from 604 to 464 islands and its extra vertices by 30 %, and `TRIM_PANEL` to 71 islands with 20 % fewer extra vertices, both passing. `frames` never worsens the worst triangle on any object and reduces `COCKPIT_SHELL_FLOOR` to 510 islands; it is the safest switch.
- **Where they hurt:** `strips` raises the worst triangle of `OVERHEAD_SHELL` from 3.10 to 11.71; `tree` raises it to 4.43; the combination reaches 4.29 there with a mean of 1.152 (limit 1.15); `tree`, `visibility` and the combination add 12 to 47 islands and 33 to 61 % extra vertices on `@MERGE_PEDESTAL_SHELL`.
- **Consequence for the design (proposal, maintainer to decide):** the gains depend on the object, so the auto-seam phase should try a few variants per object (baseline, `frames`, all) and keep the best one that passes the criteria for that object, at the cost of several runs per object.

- **Criteria reading used for the table:** a variant identical to the baseline is not a pass ("same as baseline"); "never worse than proto3" for the worst triangle is treated as a hard rule; extra vertices use `render_extra_vs_normals`.

## Session 7: Recessed Frames, Wall Corner Cuts

- **Date:** 20261019
- **Developer:** uraymeiviar
- **Agent:** Claude Code (Opus 5.5)
- **Branch:** main
- **Base Commit SHA:** d99f9a3

### Finding (maintainer observation, measured)

- `OVERHEAD_SHELL`'s largest island, a straight lattice frame around recessed panel holes, unwraps visibly skewed, with slanted outer edges and tapering bars. It is the same in proto3 and in every Session 6 variant.
- **Measured on the island** (246 triangles, 0.115 m² in 3D, one loose part):

  | Faces | Share of area | Mean area distortion | Max |
  | :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
  | front face (within 10 degrees of the main plane) | 81 % | 1.088 | 1.32 |
  | bevels (10 to 80 degrees) | 3 % | 1.12 | 1.19 |
  | side walls (about 90 degrees: frame thickness around each hole and at the outer edge) | 16 % | 1.12 | 1.42 |

- **Cause:** the walls are attached to the front face along all their edges. Flattening them into the same island forces the planar front face out of shape: a flat face should unwrap with distortion 1.0. The merge check accepted it because the island's mean (1.088) is far under the 1.25 limit.
- **The panels inside the holes are not the cause:** they are separate loose parts, sharing no vertex with the frame, that the packer placed into the holes. Islands can only connect where the mesh connects.

### Maintainer Question: Should Panels Share Vertices with the Frame?

Answer recorded (agent's assessment): **keep such panels as separate loose parts.**
- **UV:** connected panels would join the frame's island through their walls, giving a larger, non-planar island with more distortion; seams would be needed again at the same places.
- **Vertex count:** nothing would be saved. At those 90-degree edges the shading splits the vertex anyway (Weighted Normal).
- **LOD:** a loose part can be dropped on its own by the loose-piece culling of the LOD export (decision 18 of the LOD journal); a connected panel cannot.
- **Instancing:** an identical panel can become a shared mesh or an instance only if it is separate.
- **Modelling caveat (not checked):** loose parts whose borders do not meet the frame exactly can show gaps or light leaks; that is a modelling check, not a UV one.

### Auto-Seam Item (next work)

1. **Wall corner cuts for recessed holes and outer edges:** when a soft chart contains a planar face plus walls standing at more than about 60 degrees around a hole (or along an outer edge), cut the vertical corner edges where two walls meet. Each wall then stays attached to the face along one edge only and folds flat into the hole, like an opened box. The face stays exactly planar with straight bars; walls are undistorted; no extra islands; the walls use the empty space inside the holes. Verify by the usual trial unwrap and overlap check (walls folding into a small hole may overlap each other or the face; then fall back to cutting the shortest walls off as strips).
2. **Planarity check:** in the merge and final checks, compare the planar part of each island (faces within about 10 degrees of its dominant plane) with its true planar projection, and require a distortion close to 1.0 (for example a mean of at most 1.02) there, in addition to the island-wide 1.25 limit. A warped flat face can then no longer pass on the average.
3. **Test case:** `OVERHEAD_SHELL`'s largest island (above). Expected: the front face mean drops from 1.088 to about 1.0, with straight edges in the layout image. Also check every other object for islands this check now rejects, and report how many.

## Session 8: Texel Density Ranges and Hole Nesting (Packing)

- **Date:** 20261019
- **Developer:** uraymeiviar
- **Agent:** Claude Code (Opus 5.5)
- **Branch:** main
- **Base Commit SHA:** 3246181

### Observation (maintainer)

- An artist would slightly raise the texel density of a frame such as `OVERHEAD_SHELL`'s lattice (Session 7), so that the panel islands fit exactly into the frame's holes in the atlas and the holes waste no pixels. The packer of Sessions 4 and 5 keeps every island at its exact target density (Session 5 requires within 1 %), so it cannot do this.

### Maintainer Decisions (continued)

12. **Texel density is a range, not a number.** The packer may choose any density inside an island's range to fit the atlas better.
    - Default range: 1.0 to 1.1 × the base density (up to 10 % above, never below).
    - An artist mark is a range too. Examples: lower density 0.7 to 0.8; higher density for the glareshield, for example 1.5 to 1.65. This refines decision 4, whose single factor becomes a range.
13. **Below the range only by overflow:** density may drop below an island's range only through the overflow handling of decision 10 (uniform scale-down), and that is reported.

### Packing Item (next work, after the Session 5 baseline)

1. **Density as a free variable:** every island, or every stacking unit, gets a density inside its range. Objective: the smallest layout, meaning the highest overall density, or the fewest tiles. Tie-break toward the top of each range (sharper texture at no cost).
2. **Hole nesting:** for a large island with holes (frames, lattices), try a few scales inside its range so that specific small islands fit its holes with their margins. In particular, pair holes with the recessed panels cut from them, which often have almost the hole's shape (as in `OVERHEAD_SHELL`). Keep a scale only if the whole layout gets smaller.
3. **Metrics:** the chosen density per island against its range (must stay inside, except overflow), the share of hole area filled, and the share of the square used against the Session 5 baseline (fixed density).
4. **Test cases:**
   - `OVERHEAD_SHELL`'s lattice with its panels;
   - a set with one object marked 0.7 to 0.8;
   - the seven round-3 objects packed per material at 4K and 8K, as in Session 5.
5. **Session 5's first version keeps its fixed-density criterion** (within 1 %) as the baseline. This item is measured against it.

- **Date:** 20261019
- **Developer:** uraymeiviar
- **Agent:** Claude Code (Opus 5.5)

### Maintainer Decisions (continued)

14. **Decision 12 amended:** the default texel density range is **0.9 to 1.1** (10 % either way), so the packer may also make an island up to 10 % less sharp when that helps the fit.
15. **No texture size for now:** UVs are relative (0 to 1). The packer fills the square; density is relative, within each island's range. The margin is set per material as a fraction of the UV square: the mip level at which islands bleed depends only on that fraction, whatever the resolution. Texture size, absolute pixels per metre, overflow and tiles (decisions 9 and 10) are on hold, to return together as one feature if a guaranteed sharpness per material is needed.
16. **Marks are inherited:** an object without its own mark takes the nearest marked ancestor's (for example an empty grouping the rear door).
17. **Island direction options:** Free (any rotation), Upright (no rotation), Flip (0 or 180 degrees), Quarter (90-degree steps).
