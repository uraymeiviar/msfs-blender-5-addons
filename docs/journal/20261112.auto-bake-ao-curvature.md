### Objective

Bake AO, and later curvature, for cockpit parts without hand preparation, and keep the result usable after the UV
layout changes. Today the artist bakes AO by hand: the shared interaction parts (collection
`INTERACTIONS_SHARED_MESH`) are laid out on a plane and baked there, and for a panel's AO the asymmetric interaction
parts are hidden so they leave no irregular shadow. This takes a long time and has to be repeated after UV changes.

### Decisions (maintainer)

1. **A master of each map, outside the `.blend`,** like the `.visibility` file. It stays usable after UV islands are
   moved, rotated, scaled, re-unwrapped or moved to another texture set or texture size. A change of the UV layout is
   a copy from the master, never a re-bake.
2. **No hand preparation.** The auto-bake places no artificial occluders and needs nothing set up by the artist.
3. **AO, not a lightmap:** no airframe shell or other light blocker is added around the cockpit.
4. **Panels are baked with the buttons pressed,** the pose that shadows the panel least.
5. **The master is stored per island, not per face,** with many islands accepted and **no distortion at all**.
   Adjacent faces with the same normal join one island.
6. **The bake tools never change the artist's UV islands.** UVs come from the artist and the auto-packer. A density
   mismatch where two islands meet is not fixed by moving or reshaping islands.
7. **Sky light is useful,** but only for unique meshes: instanced copies share one mesh, one UV and one image.
8. **The first prototype is the master and the copy, AO only,** run as the hand-off below. The occluder automation
   follows after it.

### Proposals (not answered by the maintainer)

The hand-off follows P1 to P4 until the maintainer says otherwise.

- **P1: islands by unfolding.** An island grows from its largest free face. A neighbour across an edge joins by being
  hinged flat on that edge, so every face keeps its exact shape. A vertex may end up inside an island only when the
  face angles around it add up to 360° (flat, cylinders, cones), and the unfolded faces must not overlap. Coplanar
  joining (decision 5) is the simplest case; this rule also keeps knob sides and round button walls in one strip. The
  same mesh always gives the same islands.
- **P2: a skirt as the gutter.** Each island's border is surrounded by the neighbour faces, unfolded across the border
  edges into the gutter and baked as real surface, not by stretching border pixels. Pixels beyond a corner vertex
  (not 360°) take the nearest real face. Gutter width 2 px at master density. The skirt copy is invisible to rays so it
  casts no AO on the faces it duplicates.
- **P3: density and depth.** Master density twice the packing density, the same on every face. AO and curvature are
  one channel each, 16-bit integer (all AO is 16-bit, material decision S33.3), one image per object and per map;
  curvature is signed with 50 % grey as flat, as in Substance Painter.
- **P4: the copy into the artist's layout.** Every pixel of the target layout is sampled at several points; each point
  finds its triangle, maps through its barycentric coordinates into the master and reads it bilinearly. The sample count
  grows with the size ratio, so a small texture does not alias. The target's padding comes from extending the nearest
  triangle's mapping past its edge, which reads the real neighbour surface from the master (the island or its skirt).
  Faces whose target density is above the master's are reported.
- **P5: staleness.** Curvature is stale when the object's own mesh or normals change; AO also when an occluder within
  the AO distance, the occluder choice, the poses or the bake settings change. Separate files per map so one can be
  re-baked without the other.
- **P6: occluders without preparation.** Occluders are the real LOD0 scene minus Non-AO-Occluder objects and hidden
  kinds (collisions, clickspots, helpers, decals). Each surface is baked with moving parts in the pose that darkens it
  least: panels and bases with buttons pressed (the pose where the cap is deepest toward the panel, read from the
  animation, not from a frame number); caps released. A mover whose shape changes along its motion (pointer knobs,
  toggles, rockers, guards, the gear lever) is hidden for the panel; a round knob turning on its own axis stays an
  occluder. Shared meshes get one AO averaged over their copies; a low-resolution pre-pass measures how much the
  copies differ, and the artist chooses to keep the average, split into groups, or give them unique UV2.
- **P7: the plane for shared parts.** For a shared mesh, rays from its placed copies in their local ±X, ±Y, ±Z find the
  panel it sits on. A consistent nearest hit gives the down side and the plane height; disagreement is reported.
  Parts that sit together (cap with base, guard with guard base) are baked together on that plane.
- **P8: more maps later.** Ray-traced, with a master: sky light, cavity (AO with a short distance), thickness. Computed
  during the copy, with no master: ID map per object or merge group, world or object normal, position. Priority after
  AO and curvature: ID map, sky light, cavity.
- **P9: seams between islands of different density.** With the islands fixed (decision 6) only the sharpness can be
  evened out: near a seam, the copy reads with a footprint as large as the sparser side's pixels, fading back over a
  band. For AO this can be part of the copy; for painted maps it waits for the Substance seam investigation.

### Quality Analysis of the Copy Against a Direct Bake

A direct Cycles bake reads the true AO once per pixel, with Monte Carlo noise, and probably without anti-aliasing
(not checked). A copy reads it twice: into the master, then into the layout.

| Issue | Expected | Detection or remedy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Softening from the second read | about half a target pixel at 2× master density | bicubic reading if needed |
| Aliasing | copy better: several samples per pixel | none needed |
| Noise | copy better, and the same in every re-layout | master needs enough samples once |
| Target denser than master (scaled-up islands, larger texture, stretched unwrap) | blur where a direct bake has detail | reported per face; re-bake that object's master denser |
| Target much coarser than master | aliasing if samples are too few | sample count grows with the ratio; no master mips (a 2 px gutter vanishes at the second mip) |
| Island borders in the master | exact with the skirt, except a point-sized wedge at non-flat corners | nearest real face there |
| Target padding | equal to Blender's Adjacent Faces margin | extended mapping (P4) |
| Normals and settings | a wrong master with no visible sign if they differ | staleness covers normals, AO distance, samples, poses |
| Stacked target faces | the same as a direct bake | average or one face wins |
| Bugs: half-pixel offset, drift along long strips, skirt casting AO | obvious errors | the prototype tests each |

Expected: equal to a direct bake or better in aliasing and noise, slightly softer, worse only where the target is
denser than the master, which is detected.

#### Steps

1. **Set up.** Scripts in `tests/bake_prototype/`, output in `tests/_work/ao_bake/` (git-ignored). Use the existing
   copy `tests/_work/ao_bake/COCKPIT.blend`; save every changed scene as a new file there, never over the copy. Pick
   three objects and record their names:
   - a small flat panel with some `BTN_CAP` / `BTN_BASE` copies on it (the buttons are occluders only);
   - one `BTN_CAP` copy;
   - one rotary knob copy (a cylinder side, to test P1's unrolling).

   Occluders for every bake: all visible mesh objects whose bounding box lies within the AO distance (0.1 m) of the
   target's bounding box, in the file's current pose. No pose logic, no tags, no pre-pass in this prototype.
2. **Check the ray-invisible copy first.** Bake AO of one object directly into its own UV, then bake a coincident copy
   of it (same mesh and split normals, ray visibility off, the original left visible as occluder) into the same UV.
   They must agree within the noise of two direct bakes with different seeds. If they do not, stop and report: P2
   depends on it.
3. **Build the master** (P1, P2, P3) as a separate bake mesh: the islands by unfolding, each with its skirt, laid out
   at 2× the target's median density, packed with 2 px gutters, split normals copied from the original. Check: every
   face's master edge lengths equal its 3D edge lengths times the density (relative error at most 1e-4); no overlaps;
   the island count and the gutter's share of the master's pixels.
4. **Bake the master's AO** in Cycles into a 16-bit single-channel image; fill the wedges beyond corners from the
   nearest real face; save as 16-bit PNG with the master UV per face corner and the density in an npz beside it.
5. **Make the target layouts** on copies of the object's UV map (the map its material uses), at the real texture size
   of its material's images, or 4096 if none is found:
   - (a) unchanged;
   - (b) every island rotated 37° about its centre, then packed without scaling;
   - (c) islands scaled 0.5×; (d) scaled 1.9× (still below master density); (e) scaled 2.5× (above master density);
   - (f) a re-unwrap with different seams (Smart UV Project), scaled to the same median density;
   - (g) layout (a) at a quarter of the texture size.
6. **Copy** the master into each layout (P4), with padding of 8 px.
7. **Bake references** directly into each layout: ground truth with many samples (at least 8× the normal count), and
   a normal bake with the same samples as the master. Note whether Cycles bakes anti-aliased (jittered inside the pixel)
   or at the pixel centre only.
8. **Measure** against ground truth, over pixels inside triangles, for the copy and for the normal direct bake: mean
   absolute error, RMS, 99th percentile, maximum; the same in a 2 px band along the target's seams; and in the padding.
   Save the images and difference images as PNG for review. Record the master's disk size, the bake and copy times.
9. **Append Session 2** to this journal: the objects, the numbers per object and layout, each finish line as MET or NOT
   MET, what failed and why. Commit only the prototype scripts and this journal, by name (the working tree holds
   other people's uncommitted changes), with a conventional message, without pushing.

#### Finish Line

Report each as MET or NOT MET, with numbers.

1. **Ray-invisible copy:** step 2 agrees within the noise of two direct bakes.
2. **No distortion:** step 3's edge-length check passes for every face of the three objects; no overlaps; the knob's
   side is one island.
3. **Copy as good as a direct bake:** for layouts (a), (b), (c), (d), (f) and (g), the copy's mean absolute error and
   99th percentile against ground truth are no larger than the normal direct bake's.
4. **No seam lines:** in the seam band the copy's mean absolute error is at most 1.5× its error inside islands, and no
   line is visible in the difference images.
5. **Denser than master is detected:** in layout (e) the faces above master density are listed with their count, and
   the error there is reported.
6. **Cost:** master size per object, the gutter's share, and the times are reported.

The copy as specified (coincident, ray visibility off) does not work in Cycles. Selected to Active works without any
offset: Cycles evaluates the AO on the target's own triangle at the copy's point, with the target's self-intersection
exclusion. The master (core and skirt) is therefore baked Selected to Active from the target (extrusion 2e-5 m, ray
distance 4e-5 m), with every ray visibility of the bake meshes off. An emission bake of a 5 mm sine of the position
through master and copy (`check_mapping.py`) agreed with the direct bake to MAE 0.012 (softening only), so the
projection lands on the right faces.

Anti-aliasing: an emission bake of a sharp step gives 0 of 18642 pixels between 0 and 1 with 1 sample and 74 with
64 samples, so Cycles jitters inside the pixel (sample 0 at the centre). The copy matched GT only after its points
were kept on the pixel's own triangle (below), which fits Cycles keeping its jitter on that triangle; the Cycles source
was not read to confirm it.

### Steps 3 and 4: The Master

`step3_master.py` with `bake_unfold.py`: density = 2 x the area-weighted median density of layout (a); islands by
unfolding; skirt; shelf packing with each island's rectangle 2 px larger on every side; checks; bake of core and skirt
(64 samples, AO distance 0.1 m); compose (core pixels, then skirt pixels, then the rest of each island's rectangle from
the nearest baked pixel of the same island); 16-bit PNG and an npz (master UV per face corner in float64, island per
face, density, rectangles, face map, settings, mesh fingerprint).

P1 as implemented, and what changed on the way (an agent's choice, for the maintainer to confirm):

- A vertex may get two positions in an island; it is then on the border at both (the cut of an unrolled cylinder). The
  first implementation allowed one position per vertex and island; it could not close a cylinder's strip (the last
  quad was refused) and split the knob's side into 2 or more islands. P1's 360 degree rule still holds: a vertex is
  inside an island only when its fan closes.
- Join order: an island grows from the largest free triangle, the neighbour across the longest shared edge first.
  Smallest fold first (the other natural reading) hinges the knob's 20 side faces one by one onto its 15 degree bevel
  ring like petals (21 of 40 side edges continuous, the side in one island only formally); longest edge first unrolls
  the side as one strip (39 of 40 continuous, one cut). No fold limit is needed.
- Overlap is tested on triangles shrunk by 1e-6 about their centres (separating axes), so shared edges do not count.

### Steps 5 to 8: Layouts, Copy, References, Measurement

`step5_layouts.py` makes the layouts on copies of `UVMap` in the extracted scene (the object's own map is not
changed): (a) unchanged; (b) every UV island rotated 37 degrees about its box centre; (c), (d), (e) islands scaled 0.5,
1.9, 2.5 about their centres; (f) Smart UV Project (66 degrees) on a duplicate, scaled to (a)'s median density; (b) to
(f) shelf-packed without rotation or scaling, islands 16 px apart; (g) layout (a) at 1024. Every layout is baked into
a crop of its texture (the pixel grid unchanged). GT: direct bake, 1024 samples (16 x); direct: 64 samples (the
master's count); both with an 8 px Adjacent Faces margin. Copy: `bake_copy.py`, 8 px padding.

The copy (P4) as measured:

- Each pixel whose centre is in a triangle is sampled on an L x L grid, L per triangle = 2 x the pixel's footprint in
  master pixels (largest singular value of the layout-to-master map), at least 2, at most 16; no pixel reached 16
  except in layout (g) of the panel (816 pixels).
- Only grid points on the pixel's own triangle count, because a direct Cycles bake keeps its jitter on that triangle.
  Integrating over the whole pixel (the first version) was further from GT: panel (a) P99 0.077 against 0.048, (g)
  MAE 0.027 against 0.008.
- The master is read bilinearly, but only from texels that show a face within 30 degrees of the sampled face (the

  centre is in a face or on its border, so by that rule no inside pixel has its centre outside every face. To find
  the pixels where Cycles' rule differs, the script bakes each layout once more with margin 0 (AO, 1 sample, about
  0.02 s) and takes the pixels Cycles left unwritten.
- **open:** the pixel centre within 2 layout px of an open mesh edge (24 edges on the panel).
- **fold:** within 2 layout px of an edge whose faces differ by more than 30 degrees (with consistent winding) and lie
