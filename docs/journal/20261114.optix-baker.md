# OptiX AO Baker

The AO Bake of `fss_blender_tools` (auto-bake journal [20261112.auto-bake-ao-curvature.md](20261112.auto-bake-ao-curvature.md))
as a C++ OptiX baker in the Glider repository (`F:\glider`): the same kind of masters, many times faster, with the
Cycles bake kept as the fallback. The design is the Glider plan `doc/plan/PLAN-20261114.optix-ao-baker.md`; this
journal holds the decisions and the sessions.

## Session 1: Decisions and the Plan

- **Date:** 20261114
- **Developer:** uraymeiviar, Claude Opus 5.5
- **Base Commit SHA:** a2b7cb7 (this repository, with the uncommitted 0.76.0 working tree of the Apply copy in C++,
  not part of this work); Glider 757083b

### Maintainer Decisions

Given by the maintainer on 20261114 when the work was started.

- **Decision 1: Glider layout.** The baker is a Glider module plus a standalone app, as see++ is a standalone app
  running the image viewer's module code through a shared setup (`ImageViewerSetup`) and `glider-core.lib`. The
  module and the app share one baker core. The GUI shows the bake's progress.
- **Decision 2: IPC with the lowest latency and the highest bandwidth possible.** Mesh and image arrays go through
  shared memory (mmap). The shared-memory code goes under Glider's `src/platform` (header there, `win32/`
  implementation) as a cross-platform layer. A suggestion, not decided: the control and progress messages in a small
  ring in the same shared memory with a named wake-up event, also in `src/platform`, so the module inside
  `glider2.exe` (not started by Blender) and the app use one channel.
- **Decision 3: fallback.** Without an NVIDIA GPU the add-on keeps the current Cycles bake.
- **Decision 4: no reliance on Cycles.** No byte comparison with Cycles is required (the aircraft is never rendered
  in Cycles). A comparison with a reference is fine where one exists, but the work does not lean on it.
- **Decision 5: the falloff is computed from each ray's hit distance,** one set of rays (today: one bake per
  distance step, decision 53 of the auto-bake journal).
- **Decision 6: in the first version the master layout and the file writing stay in Python** ("before we go to
  more advanced tooling").
- **Decision 7: the curvature and the OIDN preparation move to C++.** They take most of the time that is not
  rendering. A bake log of 10 masters: Cycles 3934 s at 1024 samples, OIDN preparation 2224 s, curvature 105 s.
- **Decision 8: the step order** proposed earlier is accepted, to be refined in the plan: (a) the platform shared
  memory, the protocol and an app skeleton; (b) the AO of one static mesh; (c) occluders, poses and shared-mesh
  copies through IAS rebuilds, the falloff; (d) the curvature and the OIDN preparation, then the add-on's
  integration with the Cycles fallback.
- **Decision 9: permissions in `F:\glider` for this work.** The agent and its sub-agents may change project
  settings (`.vcxproj`, `.props`, `.sln`) or add what is required, and build and run tests without asking. Glider's
  other rules (PLAN files first, journals, approval words before plan work starts) still apply.

- **Decision 10: the AO definition is P1's option A** (maintainer, 20261114): the hemisphere around the
  interpolated shading normal (smooth or custom normals), cosine-weighted, with directions below the face counted as
  occluded, as Cycles bakes it now.
- **Decision 11: the control channel is P2** (maintainer, 20261114): two rings of messages in one shared memory
  block, one per direction, each with a named event, both process ids in the block; the data blocks are made by the
  client and named in messages. It settles decision 2's suggestion.
- **Decision 12: names and window are P3** (maintainer, 20261114): `ao-baker.module` (View/AO Baker, sources in
  `src/modules/ao-baker/`), `aobaker.exe` (`src/apps/aobaker/`, title AO Baker), namespace `fss::aobaker`; the app
  shows its window when the add-on's run mode is Foreground and runs `--headless` in Background; the module in
  `glider2.exe` serves the channel `default`.
- **Decision 13: determinism and sampling are P4** (maintainer, 20261114): every random number keyed by the master
  pixel, the sample and the view (Owen-scrambled Sobol), so a master does not depend on the tile size and a re-bake
  gives the same bytes; sample 0 at the pixel centre, the others jittered over the pixel and mirrored back into its
  triangle, as Cycles' bake does. Today's tile seeds are not used by the AO Baker.
- **Decision 14: the falloff is linear, P5** (maintainer, 20261114): a hit at distance t counts `min(t / d, 1)`, d
  the master's AO distance; the limit of today's average over n distances. The number of Falloff Steps then has no
  effect on the AO Baker; the setting only switches the falloff on (the panel's change belongs to step (d)).

Constraints given with them: nothing is written into `G:\e-jets-e2` or `F:\new-fss-aircraft-ejet` (tests run on
copies in `tests/_work/`); the uncommitted 0.76.0 working tree is not committed by this work; no `ao_bake_*.py` is
edited before the integration step, which is coordinated with the maintainer because another session hands off
"opening the master in C++" in `ao_bake_core.py` and `ao_bake_copy.py`; nothing is pushed; every C++ build and test
runs under `cpucap.exe 50`, one heavy job at a time.

### What the Current Bake Does (read for the plan)

- The runner (`ao_bake_runner.py`, a second Blender) bakes per master tile by tile. A tile is one Cycles AO bake
  (`bpy.ops.object.bake(type='AO')`, selected to active, extrusion 2e-5 m, ray distance 4e-5 m) of the core
  triangles (the target's own triangles with their master UVs) and one of the skirt triangles (pieces of the
  neighbouring or mirrored faces beyond each island border), from the target, which is a temporary mesh of the
  object's baked triangles with their corner normals. So the AO is taken at the target's own surface, with its
  shading normal, against the target itself and the occluders. The two red channels (A core, B skirt, -1 where not
  baked) go to the composition (`compose_block`: the corner rule of decision 57, the nearest fill of the gutter, the
  face map), which writes the master band by band.
- Poses (decision 34) and a shared mesh's copies (decision 47) are views: per view the target and the bake pieces
  are moved by a matrix and other occluders are shown; a copy's poses are averaged, the copies combined (Average,
  Lightest, Darkest; One Instance bakes one copy). The falloff (decision 53) bakes each view at n distances and
  averages them. The sample split (decision 49) gives each view its share of the samples.
- After the bake: the curvature master from the geometry (`write_curvature`) and the denoise of the raw master
  (`denoise_master_oidn`: per window the normals, the islands coloured so neighbours are filtered apart,
  `_dilate_labels` and `_push_pull` per group, then OIDN from Blender's library).

### Proposals (for the maintainer)

The plan states the design in full; these are its choices that need an answer, asked one at a time.

- **P1: the AO definition** (asked first). Option A: the hemisphere around the interpolated shading normal (smooth
  or custom normals), with directions below the face counted as occluded, as Cycles bakes it now. Option B: the
  hemisphere around the flat face normal. Recommended: A. The masters stay the kind they are, and the AO follows the
  shading the artist sees; B makes the AO jump at each triangle edge of a smooth low-poly surface, while A darkens
  convex smooth-shaded edges a little where the shading normal tilts beyond the face (as today's masters do).
- **P2: the control channel** of decision 2's suggestion: two rings of messages in one shared memory block, one per
  direction, each with a named event, both process ids in the block so either side notices the other's end; data
  blocks made by the client and named in messages (plan 3.2, 3.3). Recommended over a pipe or a socket: one
  mechanism for both hosts, no copy of the arrays, and Python needs only `mmap` and `ctypes`.
- **P3: names:** `ao-baker.module` (View/AO Baker) and `aobaker.exe` (`src/apps/aobaker/`), namespace
  `fss::aobaker`; the app with its window when the add-on's run mode is Foreground, `--headless` in Background.
- **P4: determinism and sampling:** every random number keyed by the master pixel, the sample and the view
  (Owen-scrambled Sobol), so a master does not depend on the tile size; samples jittered over the pixel and kept
  inside its triangle, as Cycles' bake does, sample 0 at the centre. The tile seeds of today are not used.
- **P5: falloff value:** a hit at distance t counts `min(t / d, 1)`, the limit of today's average over n distances;
  the Falloff Steps setting then only switches the falloff on.

## Session 4: Rays From a Concave Edge (P6)

- **Date:** 20261114
- **Developer:** uraymeiviar, Claude Opus 5.5

### Decision

- **Decision 16: a ray's start also moves a few float steps into its triangle** (maintainer, 20261114, "Yes, option
  A", on P6 of Session 3). The start is the point plus the face normal times the offset, plus the direction toward
  the centroid of the target triangle the piece lies on times the same offset (1e-7 + 1e-6 of the point's largest
  coordinate). A point on a concave edge then lies off the other face's plane, and its rays hit that face.
  - The centroid is the target triangle's, not the piece's: a skirt piece is a sliver beside an edge, and moving
    toward its own centroid left 25 of the box's pixels at 1/1024.

### Verified (re-run numbers)

- `test_ao_baker_static.py`: 12 of 12. The inside of the box is now 0 on all 258,693 pixels (was 2,585 at 1/1024 or
  2/1024), so finish line 3 is met. The plane is still 1 everywhere; the plane meeting a wall is within 2.37 sigma
  at its worst pixel (RMS 0.20 sigma). 9.05 G rays/s.
- `test_ao_baker_cycles.py` on `SEAT_OBS`, the Cycles master kept from Session 3: 8 of 8.
  - Owners equal at tile 2048 and 512; the master is equal at both (it differs from Session 3's, as it should).
  - Tracing 8.5 s at 8.8 G rays/s, 42 times Cycles' 355 s; the whole master 74 s against 437 s.
  - Against Cycles, the same as in Session 3 to the digits shown: mean -0.0021, mean absolute 0.0027, 99th

- `test_ao_baker_ipc.py`: 14 of 14.
- **Not verified:** occluders, more than one view, a scene larger than the GPU (as in Session 3).

## Session 6: Step (d) Begun, the Curvature in C++

### What Was Built

- **In Glider:** the curvature on the CPU (`AoCurvature`), with or without a GPU.
  - `CurvatureLoad` takes the master's triangles as the runner passes them to `write_curvature`: positions, corner
    normals and master UVs, all float64.
  - It builds `curvature_context` with the same operations in the same order: the welded sharp edges, the smooth term
    per welded vertex, and the edges' sample points.
  - Blender's KD-tree range search becomes a static KD-tree with Blender's rule: float32 points, kept when the squared
    float32 distance is at most the squared range.
  - `CurvatureRows` takes a band of the `.ao.map`'s codes and gives the 16-bit rows of `write_curvature`, split over
    threads.
- **Here, in `tests/` only:** the client's calls, and `test_ao_baker_curvature.py`. It compares the baker's rows with
  the add-on's own `curvature_context` and `curvature_at`, run by `write_curvature`'s loop over the same codes.

### Verified (re-run numbers)

- **Finish line 1, met:** the C++ curvature equals the Python one byte for byte.
  - The AO suite's 23 masters: 1,564,846 pixels, 0 differing.
  - The 10 masters of the log on the cockpit copy: 233,600,336 pixels, 0 differing.
  - The sharp edges (4 to 522 per master) and the triangle-edge candidates are equal in number on every master.
- **Time:** 5.3 s for the 10, against 94.1 s in Python, 6 to 32 times faster per master. `SEAT_OBS` takes 1.7 s
  against 34.0 s.
- `test_ao_baker_curvature.py`: 23 of 23 on the suite, 11 of 11 with `--cockpit`.

### Proposal (for the maintainer)

- **P7: where OIDN runs** (plan Section 3.11). Today the runner calls the `OpenImageDenoise.dll` Blender ships,
  through ctypes.
  - Option A: the whole OIDN window in the baker. The baker does the normals, the islands, the colouring, the
    dilation and the push-pull in C++. It then runs OIDN from that same `OpenImageDenoise.dll` of Blender, loaded by

## Session 8: The Denoise in the Baker

- **Date:** 20261115
- **Developer:** uraymeiviar, Claude Opus 5.5
- **Base Commit SHA:** a2b7cb7 (with the same uncommitted 0.76.0 working tree); Glider 757083b. The Glider side is
  in its journal `doc/journals/20261114.optix-ao-baker.md`, Session 7.

Decision 20 leaves the OIDN part to the agent; the maintainer judges the result. No `ao_bake_*.py` file was edited.

### What Was Built

- **In Glider:** the denoise of a raw master in the baker, window by window.
  - `OidnDevice` loads `oidn/OpenImageDenoise.dll` beside the baker by path, makes OIDN's GPU device (the CPU when
    there is none), and keeps one RT filter and its buffers.
  - `AoDenoise` takes the layout once (`DenoiseLoad`: corner normals, master UVs and island per face) and denoises a
    window of the raw master and its `.ao.map` codes (`DenoiseWindow`), returning the band.
  - Steps 1 to 3 are the add-on's method, `denoise_master_oidn`: the islands are split into groups at least 48 px
    apart. Per group, an image holds its islands grown 4 px with their own edge values and normals, push-pull
    fills the rest, and OIDN runs on it.
  - Steps 4 and 5 are new, after what the measurements showed.
- **Here, in `tests/` only:** the client's calls and `test_ao_baker_denoise.py`.
  - Each master is baked three times by the baker: the raw master at the job's samples, the reference at 16 times
    them with another seed, and a second raw master with a third seed for the raw master's own noise.
  - The raw master is denoised by the baker in the add-on's windows: plain OIDN, the mean restored, and the final
    version. With `--python`, the add-on's own `oidn_isolated` runs too.
  - The result is measured against the reference and written to `tests/_work/ao_baker_denoise/`: the masters as
    16-bit PNG, and crops raw | variants | denoised | reference.

### What the Measurements Showed, and Steps 4 and 5

- **At 1024 samples the raw master is already clean.** Its noise is 0.0015 to 0.0020 RMS (the second seed's
  difference agrees with the reference's to three digits, so the reference's own error is small). That is about
  half an 8-bit level. The baker's Owen-scrambled Sobol samples converge much faster than independent ones.
- **OIDN as the add-on runs it drifts.** On the 10 cockpit masters the result is 0.0002 to 0.0005 darker than the
  reference, and its 4 x 4 block error grows from 0.0005 to 0.0006 to 0.0008. On the suite's noisy masters it is 0.007
  to 0.015 lighter. White areas come out about 0.001 darker.
  - Step 4 takes the drift back: each pixel gets the mean of raw - denoised over a 33 x 33 box on its own island. The
    raw master has no drift. A group's islands lie too far apart for the box to reach another.
- **OIDN blurs AO edges that the normals do not show.** The armrests have a contact shadow inside one face (0.54 to
  0.05 between two pixels). OIDN made it 0.59, 0.38, 0.06 (0.33 off at one pixel) and turned the AO's two-pixel steps
  into ramps. The denoised armrest was worse than the raw one: RMS 0.0022 against 0.0015, worst within 16 px of an
  island's border.
  - Step 5 bounds the change: a pixel moves at most 3 noise sigmas from its raw value. The noise is
    sigma^2 = alpha x max(v (1 - v), 0.0025), with alpha measured per window from what OIDN took away (the median
    absolute deviation). A noisy master gets a wide bound, a clean one a narrow bound.
