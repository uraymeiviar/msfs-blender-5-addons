### Where This Stands

- The break the artist fixes by hand is already there in Substance Painter, before any export. So the 16-bit UVs of the simulator package are not its cause, assuming Substance loads the original UVs (not checked).
- The most likely cause is the triplanar projection the artists use, which switches direction on curved surfaces. This is strongly indicated, not confirmed: it has not been checked on the exact spot of the artist's report, or against the UV seams.
- No tool is decided. The automatic padding tool and placing small islands in accurate UV zones are on hold as fixes for this problem.
- Nothing was decided by the maintainer here beyond the two agreements below. All causes and fixes in this session are proposals.

### The Report, in the Artist's Words as Relayed by the Maintainer

What the maintainer relayed, in order. Some of it went through translation, so it may need checking with the artist directly.

- A line that is straight on the UV does not always stay straight on the 3D model, even with correct geometry.
- Islands that are close on the mesh must also be close on the UV. The maintainer corrected the first reading of this: it is not about straight lines, but about the seam, where the two split islands do not fit seamlessly when they join again on the mesh.
- The fix is by hand: "borrowing" an edge strip of each island of a pair and patching it onto the other, on both the outer and the inner side of the edge. This is called stamping.
- All of it is done in Substance Painter (an earlier mention of Photoshop was corrected), on a new layer, for ORM and normal as well as colour, painting on the 3D model.
- The seam is **already broken in Substance before the patch work**.
- It happens in **all channels**: albedo, ORM, normal.
- The artists use **triplanar projection**.

The maintainer's reason for caring: this hand work is why the auto packer was started, and it should go away.

### Pictures Received (Phone Photos of the Artist's Screen, Not Kept in the Repository)

1. **Real material, circled seam.** A bluish, mottled, curved part. The maintainer pointed out that the seam meant is the **more vertical** one, not the horizontal line beside it. A bright streak runs close to that seam on one side, with a bright patch beside it on the other. They may be one feature shifted sideways; the size of the shift cannot be read through the moire.
2. **Checker fill, no hardness stated.** Whole squares cut and shifted, far beyond a pixel or two, with irregular and stair-stepped cut lines. The large surface facing one way stays continuous; the strongly curved parts are chopped up.
3. **Checker, default hardness.** The breaks are soft, blurred bands where one projection fades into another. The checker does not line up across a band.
4. **Checker, maximum hardness.** The same areas are hard, jagged cuts.

In pictures 3 and 4 thin dashed lines carry on across the black background, so they look like a viewport overlay, not seams. Some cuts around small pointed shapes stay sharp in both, possibly hard edges on the model.

The artist was not asked in a way that confirms the checker layer has no mask and is set to Tri-planar; that is assumed.

### What the Pictures Show

- Hardness changes the breaks, so those breaks come from the projection switching direction: a UV seam would not respond to it.
- Lowering Hardness is **not a full fix**: it turns a cut into a blur, and the pattern still does not line up. Three flat projections cannot wrap a curved part without a mismatch somewhere.
- Hypothesis: the streak in picture 1 may be one feature seen twice, once from each projection, fading into each other. That would explain the bright patch beside the streak and all channels breaking together. If so, stamping is what fixes what triplanar cannot, and the island layout plays no part.

### Causes, as They Stand

| Cause | Status | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 16-bit UV rounding | Ruled out as the main cause | Happens at package build, after Substance; breaks are much larger than 1 to 2 px. Still real and adds a little in the simulator |
| Reflection or lighting | Ruled out as the main cause | Albedo breaks too |
| Pattern projected by UV | Ruled out, if the layer is Tri-planar | Tri-planar follows 3D position |
| Triplanar direction switch | **Most likely** | Hardness changes the breaks |
| Mask or generator from baked maps | Open | Could add to it on real materials; the checker layer has none |
| Split or faceted normals | Open | Could explain the cuts that stay sharp and the stair-steps; how Substance's triplanar uses normals is reasoned from triplanar in general, not checked |
| Pixel grid (each island samples its own pixels) | Open, small | About a pixel at most |

### 16-bit UVs: Measured, Separate from the Above

The package tool stores UVs in 2 bytes. The SDK says so, and the steps at 4096 px are: |uv| 0.25 to 0.5, 1 px; 0.5 to 1, 2 px; 1 to 2, 4 px. glTF V is 1 minus Blender V, so stored (0, 0) is Blender's top-left and the worst stored corner (1, 1) is Blender's bottom-right. The corner comes only from the format and the exporter code: the artist named no corner.

**E2-190 cockpit, source against packaged glTF (`E2-190-Cockpit_LOD00.gltf`, read only):**

- All 894 packaged TEXCOORD_0 accessors are componentType 5122, 2 bytes, read as half floats.
- Negative UVs survive (35,433 U, 10,510 V, range -10 to +10).
- Whether V is flipped is only a hint: 18.8 % of values match unflipped, 3.5 % flipped; the source is newer than the package.
- The packaged file has no samplers, so glTF's default repeat applies; what MSFS does is not confirmed.

**Seam gap through rounding, at 4096 px.** A seam pair is two vertices of a primitive with the same position and different UVs; the gap is how far rounding moves them apart relative to each other. 110,526 pairs:

| Furthest side in abs(uv) | Mean gap (px) |
| :--- | ---: |
| 0 to 0.25 | 0.14 |
| 0.25 to 0.5 | 0.39 |
| 0.5 to 1 (80,571 pairs) | 0.76 |
| 1 to 2 | 1.16 |
| above 2 | 3.62 |

All pairs: mean 0.783 px, 95 % 1.841, 99 % 2.854, maximum 25.675 (on the tiling `Seat_Belt`); above 1 px 28.5 %, above 2 px 3.1 %.

Grid pictures in the glTF viewer (`H:\git-repos\gltf-viewer`, `Glider.exe --capture`) were inconclusive: a UV grid is not continuous across seams anyway, and the camera was too far.

4. On the real material, note the streak layer's projection, mask and generators, and turn the masks off one by one.
5. Check that Substance loads the original mesh, not a packaged one.
6. Ask the object's name, so its normals along any remaining seam can be read in Blender without changing the file.
7. Take real screenshots rather than phone photos: moire hides the pixels, and the size of a shift in pixels is what decides whether blending could work.

**How to read the result:**

- A band on the same spot: it is triplanar. The answer lies in how materials are projected (which projection on which part, a lower hardness on noisy materials, hand work where a directional feature crosses a switch), not in the layout or a seam tool. No tool may be needed; the artists decide.
- A clean break on a seam with no band: a real seam problem remains; then look at normals and bakes.
- Fixing normals would mean changing the model in Blender and re-baking, which can shift painted wear, so try one object first. Hard edges may be intended.
