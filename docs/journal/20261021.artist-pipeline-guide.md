### Context

`docs/user/README.md` leads with **The Pipeline** (UV journal Session 68): ten steps for 3D artists who already know Blender, with what MSFS needs on top, which tool helps, and how to do it by hand where the tool is in development. This journal takes over the decisions about the guide from the UV journal, whose session numbers are used by the visibility prototype hand-offs.

### Maintainer Decisions

1. **The guide explains reasoning and common sense, not rigid rules.** The pipeline's order follows what each step needs from the ones before it; it is not a fixed sequence. Steps before texturing are cheap to redo, and artists go back and forth; texturing is the point where changes start to cost rework (bakes, carrying the painting over).
2. **Each step gets a "Redo when" line,** after Why, Do and Check: what makes the step worth doing again, and what that costs. An intro paragraph states decision 1.
3. **Packing may be done early and repeated.** Zone weights, density marks and visibility scores can change at any time, and packing is quick to run again; the artist may pack after unwrapping (to review density or try a texture) and pack again later. `uv.md`'s workflow ends with unwrapping and says so.
4. **Merge groups are explained by their purpose, not by a placement rule.** `@MERGE_` defers the join to the export, so parts stay individually editable; the artist decides what to merge by reducing draw calls, and keeps separate what has a life of its own (animated parts, nodes the model XML refers to). The former "put `@MERGE_` on a high level of the hierarchy" was misleading: a high position joins more, but it is not the objective. The rules themselves stay in the MSFS Authoring Guide (`modeling.md`, "When to Merge"); the step links to them. A merge group must stay inside one zone (UV journal decision 34); nothing checks this yet.
5. **An overview table** after the intro: step, tools, tool status, MSFS Authoring Guide page. Pages were matched after reading them: zones to `materials.md` and `texturing.md` (separate textures along visibility boundaries), visibility to `uv-mapping.md` (Texel Density), units to `lods.md` (background only).
6. **Animation, clickspots and collision get no step:** they are modelling work that touches several steps. A section "Alongside the Pipeline" explains how. Collision and clickspot meshes are recognised by MSFS by their invisible material type; seams and UVs on them have no effect, so skipping them is only a time saving and unwrapping them does no harm.
7. **Baking belongs to texturing (step 9):** bake once the texture set's packing is final; float moving parts away; leave clickspots, collision and decals out; instanced parts get generic AO (`baking.md`, AO Versus Instancing).
8. **Terms: mark, name prefix, tag.** A mark tells an automatic tool what it cannot work out itself (an override or hint) and lives only in the `.blend`; LOD marks, UV marks and tags are its three kinds (a tag is a mark the artist defines, classifying objects). Name prefixes (`@MERGE_`, `@NOMERGE_`, planned `@ZONE_`) are a separate term; `fss-tools.md`'s heading "Markers" becomes "Name Prefixes".
9. **Clean-ups agreed in the same review:** `lod.md` states once that files are saved without the preview, and its pitfall becomes the remaining real case (exporting by hand with **Excluded: Hidden**); `uv.md` gets a title covering both panels, plain wording instead of promotional phrases, and an artist's explanation of Distortion instead of the singular-value formula.

### Verified While Writing

- **Distortion** is σ₁/σ₂ of each face's 3D-to-UV Jacobian (`uv_heatmap.py`, SVD), so a uniform scale gives 1.0, as the new text says.
- **The origins warning for an interior needs the exterior diameter:** without it (and without an explicit minSize table), `lod_rules.min_sizes` raises "exterior diameter missing", Refresh is cancelled and nothing is measured. Step 1's Check says so.
- The visibility text in step 7 and decisions 69 and 78 to 82 (UV journal) were re-read after other sessions changed step 7; the animation sentence keeps decision 69 (0, 50 and 100 % of each animation's own length by default, more states as an option).
